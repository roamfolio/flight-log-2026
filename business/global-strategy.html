Strategy briefing · November 2026

Year in Maps: where to grow, and how to earn fairly

A plain-language strategy report for the free flight log, travel story and food map makers: who else is in the market, which countries to try first, who might pay, which business model fits, and the legal checks to do before taking money.

Products: Flight Log maker · US/World travel story · World Food Map
Facts carry a numbered source. Anything marked estimate is my own assumption for planning, not a sourced figure. This is not legal or tax advice.

Summary

  1. The demand for year-end recaps is real and huge, but mostly captured inside big apps. Spotify Wrapped 2025 reached 200 million users and 500 million shares in its first day.1 Flighty, Polarsteps and American Airlines all ship their own recaps.2,5,7 Your opening is the person whose year is spread across many apps, or who uses none of them.

  2. Own the gap: one place for all your maps, no app, no account, beautiful Story pictures. Competitors recap only what happened inside their app (Flighty: flights; Polarsteps: tracked trips; Beli: restaurants). None combines flights, places and food into one shareable set.

  3. Start with Taiwan plus the US, in Traditional Chinese and English. Taiwanese made a record 18.9 million trips abroad in 2025,10 Taiwan sent the largest share of Threads traffic in late 2025,11 and you are native to the language and culture. The US is where print-on-demand works best and where you live.

  4. Stay free; earn from prints, tips and group recaps. A subscription app would compete head-on with funded apps. Expect small numbers at first: a holiday test that earns a few hundred dollars would be a good result, not a failure. estimate

  5. Before any money changes hands: remove airline logos from anything sold, sell through a merchant-of-record platform so it handles sales tax and VAT, keep the "your data never leaves your browser" design, and confirm your own work authorization if you are on a visa.

01Market and competitors

"Year in review" is now a standard end-of-year ritual. Spotify reported Wrapped 2025 reached 200 million engaged users within 24 hours (it took about 62 hours in 2024) and over 500 million shares on day one.1 Travel apps copied the format: Flighty publishes a yearly "Flighty Passport",2 Polarsteps launched "Unpacked" in its app on 15 December 2025,5 and American Airlines launched "Cleared for Landing", a Wrapped-style report for its flyers.7

The gap you can own

  • All your maps in one set: flights, states and countries, food. Nobody else combines them.
  • No app, no account, works for any airline: people can make a recap even if they never used Flighty or Polarsteps. Import through their own AI assistant is unusual and helpful.
  • Design-first Story pictures: 5+ styles, sized for Instagram Stories, with one-tap sharing to Threads and LINE, which matter in Asia and which the US-built apps rarely prioritize.
  • Privacy by design: the makers run in the browser. That is a selling point and a legal advantage (see section 05).

Main threat: the big apps launch their recaps in December with marketing budgets you don't have. Position yours as the one that puts them all together, not as a replacement.

02Which countries first

Rank by three questions: do people there share year-end pictures on Stories, Threads or LINE; can you speak to them well; and can you deliver a print there at a fair price?

TPE

Taiwan (and Taiwanese abroad)

Start now
Why
Record 18.9 million outbound trips in 2025, up about 12%; Japan was the top destination (6.73M), then China and Korea.10 Taiwan produced the largest share of Threads web traffic in September 202511 and was named one of the three most active Threads countries in 2024.11 About 23,000 Taiwanese students are in the US.13
Language
Traditional Chinese (繁體中文) for the maker, Story text and captions. Your native advantage; few competitors do this well.
Platforms
Instagram Stories, Threads, LINE (you already have one-tap sharing to all three).
Payments
Cards through a merchant-of-record checkout. Local wallets (LINE Pay, JKOPay) are popular, but add them only if demand appears.
Prints
I could not confirm local print-on-demand production in Taiwan with Printful or Gelato. Prints may ship from Japan or the US, so check delivery time and shipping cost before promising holiday delivery. Lead with digital extras here.
SFO

United States

Start now
Why
Where you live and can test fast. Recap culture is strongest here (Wrapped, Flighty, Beli's Gen Z growth9). Your flight log and US states map already fit US travelers.
Language
English (done).
Platforms
Instagram Stories, TikTok, Threads.
Prints
Best coverage: Printful runs its own facilities in Dallas and Charlotte plus Toronto and Tijuana,14 and Gelato produces locally in 32 countries.15 Fastest holiday delivery is here.
NRT

Japan

6–12 months
Why
LINE reached 100 million monthly users in Japan in December 2025;16 Japan was also among the three most active Threads countries in 2024.11 It is Taiwanese travelers' top destination, so a Taiwan launch reaches Japan trips naturally.
Language
Japanese needs a native-quality translation; machine translation will hurt trust.
Prints
Printful works with a partner facility in Japan for selected products.14
Law
APPI applies to foreign businesses serving people in Japan.19
LHR

UK and EU

6–12 months, English first
Why
Strong travel and print-book culture (Polarsteps is Dutch and reports 20M+ travelers4). Great print coverage: Printful in the UK, Latvia and Spain;14 Gelato across Europe.15
Watch out
Polarsteps is the home favorite; GDPR rules apply once you sell to or track EU users (section 05).
SYD

Australia

Later
Why
English-speaking, heavy long-haul flyers; Printful lists partner production in Australia.14 No localization needed beyond dates and units.
ICNBKK

Korea and Thailand

Later
Why
Thailand: LINE is used by over 80% of the country.16 Korea: big Instagram culture, but KakaoTalk is the main messenger, so it needs its own share button and Korean copy.
Watch out
Each needs a native-language version and local payment habits; Korea's PIPA has extra rules for overseas businesses.18 Revisit after Japan.

Realistic first-season numbers estimate

These are planning assumptions to test, not forecasts. If the holiday push brings 1,000–3,000 people who finish a map, and 1–2% buy a print with $4–8 left after the print shop's cost, that is roughly 10–60 orders and $40–480. Add a few tips and maybe one or two group recaps. Use the test to learn which segment and which country responds, not to judge the idea on dollars alone.

Fair-price rule: set each print price as the print provider's base cost plus shipping plus a small, stated margin. Show it ("$12 = $8 print + $4 to keep this free") so buyers see it is fair.

Go signals

  • Print or extra conversion at or above 1% of makers suggested
  • People share without being asked (shares per maker rising)
  • At least 2–3 groups ask about a group recap
  • One country clearly outperforms; focus there

Stop or change signals

  • Many makers, almost no orders: keep it free as a portfolio piece; test digital extras instead of prints
  • Refunds or complaints about print quality or delivery time
  • Time spent on support exceeds what the income justifies for you

05Legal and compliance checklist

AreaWhat appliesWhat to do
Airline logosPutting a company's logo on products you sell is generally treated as trademark infringement, not fair use, and airlines enforce their marks.20No airline logos on prints, posters, paid extras or ads. Use neutral text badges (airline name or code). Consider the same for the free tool once you earn money from it.
Map dataBase maps carry their own licenses (some require attribution, some are public domain).Check the license of every map source you use and add the required credit to prints.
EU and UK (GDPR, UK GDPR)Applies to non-EU businesses that offer goods or services to people in the EU or monitor them; such businesses may need an EU representative (Art. 27).19Keep processing in the browser; collect only what checkout needs; privacy notice; avoid tracking cookies, or ask consent.
Taiwan (PDPA)Amended in 2025 to create a Personal Data Protection Commission; effective date set by the Executive Yuan.18Privacy notice in Traditional Chinese; recheck when the commission starts work.
Japan (APPI)Reaches foreign operators handling data of people in Japan when providing goods or services there.19Same minimal-data approach; Japanese privacy notice at launch.
Korea (PIPA)Rules for overseas businesses' local representatives changed in October 2025.18Get advice before targeting Korea.
US state privacy lawsAbout 20 states have comprehensive laws; most apply only above thresholds such as 100,000 consumers, but a few have none.21You are likely below most thresholds; still publish a privacy notice and honor deletion requests.
Sales tax and VATDigital sales trigger VAT abroad (e.g. Taiwan requires foreign e-service sellers to register above NT$600,000 a year, raised from NT$480,000 in April 2025).22Sell digital items through a merchant of record that collects and pays these taxes: Lemon Squeezy (about 5% + 50¢) or Gumroad (10%, merchant of record since Jan 2025).23 For prints, check which taxes your print provider collects in each country.
TipsKo-fi takes no fee on tips by many accounts, but fee settings vary; Buy Me a Coffee takes 5%.24Confirm current fees on the official page before linking.
Consumer lawEU/UK buyers have withdrawal rights for many online purchases; personalized goods are often treated differently.Clear refund policy for misprints; state that personalized prints can't be returned for change of mind, where the law allows.
Your work authorizationFor F-1 students, actively running an online business is generally treated as self-employment that needs authorization (for example OPT related to your degree); STEM OPT does not allow self-employment.25If you hold any US visa, ask your school's international office (DSO) or an immigration attorney before you earn any income, including tips. Until cleared, keep everything free.
